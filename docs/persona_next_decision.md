## Recommended option: one fixed stronger-magnitude diagnostic

Reuse the saved directions, without new training or extraction. Fix zero-based
block 17 before outputs, following the earlier registered tie-break block rather
than choosing a block from successful behavior. For each trait, try three shift
norms: 0.3, 0.6 and 1.0 times its recorded mean per-token residual norm at that
block. These are **per-token norm units**, unlike the earlier beta times neutral
response-mean norm. Include one saved random direction matched to each trait's
largest shift. Use the same 16 development prompts and one fresh common baseline.

This is 12 modified conditions plus baseline: **208 generations**, **26 blinded
eight-item batches**, and **at most 52 grading invocations** with two structural
attempts per batch. Score all three traits, ambiguity, content, coherence,
degeneration and lexical profanity. No two conditions of the same prompt share
a judging batch. There is no selection, confirmation or adaptive extension.
A concrete protocol, implementation acceptance and registration still precede
any execution after the user's choice.

The comparison asks whether larger shifts elicit any target signal. It cannot
decisively separate weak scaling from a wrong contrast, block or task context.
Degradation without a target does not prove a direction is nonspecific; a random
control only at the maximum does not establish specificity at lower strengths.
Any positive signal remains exploratory and needs later confirmation and quality
checks. Larger shifts may leave the rating calibration's clear-example domain.

Sarcasm has a particular limitation: the shared tasks are mostly neutral factual
requests, whereas extraction uses ironic praise of malfunctioning objects.
They offer limited natural opportunities for situational irony. A later revised
study could register contexts that afford irony before generating outputs.
